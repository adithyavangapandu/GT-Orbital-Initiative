About

A spacecraft built by students.

The Georgia Tech Orbital Initiative is a new student organization focused on small satellites. Members take a spacecraft through its whole life: requirements, design reviews, fabrication, environmental testing, and operations in orbit.

CubeSats are small enough for a student team to build and complex enough to need every discipline a full-size spacecraft does. That makes them the fastest way into space engineering.

  • Every major, every year. Undergrad and grad students welcome.
  • No experience required. We teach soldering, CAD, and flight software from scratch.
  • Real hardware on your résumé. Present design reviews and build flight-style systems.
Why we started

Georgia Tech has a rocket team. Undergrads don't have a satellite team.

Georgia Tech students can launch liquid-fueled rockets, fly high-altitude balloons, and study the night sky through student-run clubs. Until now, there was no student-run organization for undergraduates who want to build satellites.

Undergrads interested in spacecraft have mostly had to land a research position in a faculty lab, and those spots aren't open to everyone. We started the Orbital Initiative so any student who wants to build a satellite has a place to do it.

Programs

What we're building.

Three programs that take a new member from a workbench to a satellite in orbit.

Concept phase

Flagship CubeSat

Our first satellite: a 3U CubeSat designed, built, and tested by students. Mission selection is underway, and founding members help choose the payload.

Starting Fall 2026

FlatSat Lab

Every subsystem of a real satellite laid out on a lab bench. New members learn soldering, embedded code, and integration on hardware they can touch.

Planned

Campus Ground Station

A UHF/VHF station on campus that tracks and receives signals from satellites already in orbit, and one day from our own.

Teams

Eight subsystem teams. One spacecraft.

Members join a subsystem, the same way the space industry organizes a spacecraft program. You can switch teams at any time.

STR

Structures & Thermal

Chassis design, CAD, launch-load analysis, and thermal models for sunlight and eclipse.

AE · ME · MSE
EPS

Electrical Power

Solar cells, battery packs, and power regulation for a spacecraft that runs on a few watts.

EE · CmpE · ME
ADCS

Roadmap

The path to orbit.

We follow the NASA project life cycle, the same phases and design reviews used on professional missions.

  1. Pre-Phase A

    Build the team

    Recruit founding members, run skills workshops, and explore mission concepts.

    Now · Fall 2026
  2. Phase A

    Concept & trades

    Pick the mission, set requirements, and assemble the FlatSat.

    Target · Spring 2027
  3. Phase B

    Preliminary design

    Size every subsystem, select components, and pass PDR.

    Target · Fall 2027
  4. Phase C

    Final design

    Build an engineering model, finalize software, and pass CDR.

    Target · Spring 2028
  5. Phase D

    Build & test

    Assemble flight hardware and qualify it in vibration and thermal-vacuum tests.

    Target · 2028–29
  6. Phase E

    Launch & operate

    Fly on a rideshare or through NASA's CubeSat Launch Initiative.

    Launch · TBD

Dates are targets and depend on team size, funding, and launch availability. Joining early means you help shape the mission.
